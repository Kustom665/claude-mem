# What this niche needs from software

Mobile notaries and loan signing agents, Pennsylvania first. **As of 13 October
2026.** Written to decide what to build next, not to describe what exists.

## The short version

1. **Bookkeeping, journals, mileage and invoicing are crowded and cheap.** The
   price anchors are $8.99 to $15 a month, ratings are 4.8 to 4.9 stars, and a
   free tier exists. A generic version of those is not a business. What
   separates this product is the part nobody else does well: a tamper-evident
   journal, Pennsylvania's rules enforced rather than documented, and the tax
   split.
2. **The biggest unmet pains are about money and attention, not record-keeping:**
   getting paid late or never, deciding whether an offer is worth its hours, and
   staying credentialed and onboarded across a dozen vendor lists.
3. **Nobody publishes what the work pays**, the National Notary Association will
   not recommend fees (antitrust), and a recurring complaint is lowball offers
   and decent ones that vanish within minutes. A tool that tells a notary *their
   own* net dollars per hour for a specific offer has no competitor that was
   found.
4. **Pennsylvania changed on 28 March 2026** — a $25,000 bond, a seven-digit
   commission ID on the stamp, new fees, a ban on personal identifiers in the
   journal. Rules that change are the one durable reason to prefer a
   state-specific tool, and the one thing a generic app does badly.
5. **Built this round from it:** *Find work* (where the work comes from and how to
   get on each vendor list, scored against your own credentials, with a pipeline
   that remembers to follow up) and a public *request link* (people find and ask
   for you directly, with the Pennsylvania non-attorney statement printed on the
   page).
6. **Build next, in order:** an offer check (net dollars and dollars per hour for
   an offer, plus a counter-offer log), a get-paid autopilot (aging, reminders,
   cancellation invoices), and a one-click vendor packet.

## The landscape

### Training and certification

Loan Signing System's flagship course is $347 (pages disagree: $349, $497, $547
elsewhere), Notary2Pro Elite is $300 once, and NNA's signing-agent package is
$199 (with $294, $299 and $394 appearing on older pages). Certification is
annual. NNA says it is not required by law, though many companies require it.

### How offers arrive, and what notaries dislike

- **Snapdocs Notary Connect** is free to join and does not set or pay the fee —
  the hiring company does. Offers go down a ranked list by text and email; Snapdocs
  says assignment is not first-come-first-served, forum posters say it is. A 1–5
  score appears after 25 orders, **company feedback is not shown to the notary**,
  and no appeal route was found. VendorPay pays notaries only for companies
  enrolled in it, 1–3 weeks after close; otherwise the notary is told to chase the
  company.
- **SigningOrder** charges companies $3 an order; notaries find it easier than
  Snapdocs. **NotaryGo** offers of $30–$60 are reported. **NotaryDash** is a
  platform, not the payer. **The Closing Exchange** draws complaints about fees.
- **Directories** — Notary Rotary ($79–$99 a year Premier, free Basic), 123notary
  ($59–$99; top slots quoted individually), SigningAgent.com (with NNA
  certification). Return on paid listings is mixed on the forum, and cheap
  pay-to-list sites draw "no assignments" complaints.
- **Remote platforms:** Proof pays on-demand notaries $5 for a first seal and $25
  per real-estate closing (Pennsylvania is live); NotaryCam $99 onboarding and
  $15 a month; BlueNotary's free plan pays $22 a notarization. Pennsylvania
  requires a vendor on its approved list (updated 30 April 2026).

### Costs of starting in Pennsylvania

$42 application, $65 exam, a required three-hour course (NNA's is $85), recording
$32–$110, seal about $30, journal $30–$55 — NNA estimates $314–$417. Bond is
$25,000 for anyone appointed or reappointed on or after 28 March 2026 (was
$10,000). Signing-agent E&O costs far more than notary E&O ($275–$390 a year
reported) and does not cover signing-agent mistakes such as a wrong payoff.

## What notaries complain about

Strength is the number and independence of sources, not importance.

| | Pain | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Late or no payment.** 30 to 60+ days is reported for several companies; escalation is manual (three emails, a title-company chase, a complaint, a certified letter). | Strong, four or more sources; some report fast payment |
| P2 | **No-shows and cancellations.** The forum norm is a print fee plus a trip fee, or about half the fee; some payers pay nothing. | Strong |
| P3 | **Lowball fees.** Posters report fees "essentially the same as in 2014", a $45 refinance, and a $70 refinance gone in under two minutes. One worked a $75 fee on a 150-page refinance to about $4.62 an hour. NNA's 2023 survey reported a "significant slowdown". | Strong, anecdotal |
| P4 | **Offer overload.** The same job offered by two platforms at different fees; two offers for one time slot. | Moderate |
| P5 | **Printing and scan-backs.** Payers resist add-on pricing; $0.49 a page at a copy shop; scan-back jobs unpaid for years. | Strong |
| P6 | **Compliance mistakes and fraud.** NNA's E&O examples are a missing date, a missing borrower name, failing to identify the signer; 2026 bulletins warn of fake-check and refinance fraud. | Moderate |
| P7 | **Credential churn.** Annual re-screening, E&O certificates for each company, and in Pennsylvania a 45-day recording step, Philadelphia registration and vendor-tied e-notary approval. | Moderate |

| P9 | **Vendor-list approvals** that produce no calls, and unresponsive vendor management. | Moderate |
| P10 | **Reputation.** Hidden company feedback and no appeal. | Moderate |
| P11 | **Seasonality and oversaturation.** NNA says the stable earners diversified. | Moderate |
| P12 | **Safety.** Advice exists; no incident data found. | Weak |
| P13 | **Pay-to-play and scams.** | Moderate |
| P14 | **Duplicate data entry** between platforms and QuickBooks. | Weak to moderate |

**Where Notary Desk is genuinely different:** the sealed journal with chain

## What to build next

1. **Offer check (idea 2).** Inputs: fee offered, document pages, print cost per
   page, miles (valued at the IRS rate for that day), scan-backs, expected time.
   Outputs: net dollars and dollars per hour, and the fee that reaches the
   notary's own target. A log of accepted, declined and countered offers per payer.
   *Why first:* no competitor was found, it is small, and it is a good free tool
   to bring people in. *Constraint:* keep outputs personal. A shared price
   database invites the antitrust concern that makes NNA decline to recommend
   fees; take legal advice before building one.
2. **Get-paid autopilot (idea 1).** Aging by payer, per-payer terms, reminder
   schedule, escalation templates, and a cancellation invoice (print fee plus
   trip fee) with the timestamps from the signing. *Why second:* it is the most
   strongly evidenced pain, and the invoicing foundation is already there. The
   notary's own history of who pays slowly is private; publishing it (idea 7) is
   a different decision.
3. **Vendor packet (idea 4).** One click to the bundle every onboarding portal
   asks for, built from what the app already knows, plus E&O certificate-holder
   requests and the annual re-screening date. It turns the Find work readiness
   score from "here is what is missing" into "here is the file to upload".
4. **Pennsylvania clock (idea 5, small).** Remind the 45-day recording deadline
   (bond, oath and commission to the county Recorder of Deeds, or the commission
   is void), flag a bond below $25,000 for commissions from 28 March 2026, track
   remote-notarization vendor notifications, and print the itemized receipt the
   regulation requires.
5. **Lead polish (idea 3).** The `request.created` and `signing.completed`
   events already leave the app; ship GoHighLevel workflow recipes for a
   missed-call text-back and a review request, before writing any of it in-app.

## Learning what notaries want without being one

- **Listen first, free:** read NotaryCafe's forum (independent), NNA's notary
  bulletin, and the Pennsylvania Association of Notaries' pages. Search them for
  the pains above and count the *complaints*, not the praise.
- **Ask ten Pennsylvania notaries five things:** what did your last offer pay and
  what did it cost you to do; which company paid slowest and what did you do; what
  do you re-type; what did you wish you had to hand when you applied; what
  changed in March.
- **Test with the smallest thing:** put the offer check on a public page with no
  sign-up, and measure how many people use it and come back. That answers "does
  anyone want this" in a week without building the paid product.
- **Do not copy competitor claims.** User counts, savings figures and "5x more
  business" are marketing. The one place the verifiable evidence is strong is the
  regulation.

## Market size

- **US notaries:** about 4.44 million commissions in NNA's 2022 census (Texas
  484,230, Florida 474,670), against 4.48 million five years earlier. These are
  commission holders, not working notaries.
- **Pennsylvania:** the Department of State says "more than 85,000" are
  commissioned (undated); Wikipedia says 74,000+ and 2,137 e-notaries (undated);
  NNA's census says Pennsylvania had the largest decline of 2017–2022, 41,612.
  **These do not reconcile.**
- **Loan signing agents:** no published count. Vendor claims: Snapdocs 140,000+
  notaries and 60,000+ signings a month, CloseWise 140,000+ (the same number,
  suspiciously), NotaryGadget "tens of thousands" of users.
- **Pay:** NNA's 2020 survey said over half of full-time mobile notaries earn
  $2,000+ a month and 16% earn above $7,500; its 2023 survey (932 mobile notaries
  and signing agents) found 33% full-time and 67% part-time. Neither is a basis
  for an income promise, and the app makes none.

## What could not be verified, and decides bets

1. **Whether any product ingests offers from Snapdocs, SigningOrder or NotaryGo
   into one inbox with conflict detection.** Install SigningOS and see (an
   afternoon). This decides whether idea 6 is open ground.
2. **How many loan signing agents there are**, in the US and Pennsylvania, and
   Pennsylvania's true current notary count.
3. **Current NNA prices** and the annual prices for NotaryGadget and VenBooks.
4. **Any real user or revenue figure for a competitor**, and any market-wide data
   on 2025–26 median signing fees, volume or days-to-pay.
5. **The primary text** of Pennsylvania's March 2026 regulation (the personal
   identifier ban, itemized receipts, certified journal copies, the finality of
   the $20 electronic-notarization fee) and of the 2026 mid-year IRS mileage
   change to 76 cents. All came from summaries.
6. **Whether Pennsylvania loan signings need an attorney present** — NNA's own
   pages disagree.
7. **Whether advance disclosure of travel and clerical fees is legally mandatory**
   in Pennsylvania, or only the Department's guidance.
8. **Notary-side terms** for SigningOrder, Rocket Close, ServiceLink and title
   underwriters, and any standard payment or cancellation terms.
9. **Any route in, or pay evidence,** for law firms, hospitals and care homes,
   banks, real-estate firms, courts and jails.
10. **Which of the Department's 32+ listed vendors** are approved for remote
    notarization rather than electronic notarization only.
